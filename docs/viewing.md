# How to View Stereograms

Instead of looking _at_ the image, look _through_ the image, as if you were looking through a window at the landscape outside. Contrary to some, most stereograms don't need you to cross your eyes, but rather do the opposite—unfocus your eyes so that they look _behind_ the stereogram.

Doing this on command can take practice. However, there are some methods that can help you get started.

## The Reflection Method

1. Open the stereogram on a large screen like a laptop or PC monitor, ideally with a glossy screen.
2. Turn on your phone's flashlight and point it at the screen (3-4 inches away) so that you can see the light's reflection in the middle of the stereogram.
3. Focus on the light. You should only see one light—if you see two, dim the screen until you are able to focus on the flashlight, then turn the screen back on.
4. Slowly bring your phone away from the screen towards you, making sure to keep your focus on the light.
5. Keep doing this until you see a 3D image begin to emerge. You may need to move the light very far depending on the stereogram—sometimes even behind your head.

These steps should help you focus on a point behind the image. With enough practice, you'll be able to see stereograms without the help of a reflection.

## The Close-Up Method

If the previous method doesn't work, or if the stereogram is on a matte surface like a book, you can try this instead:

1. Bring the stereogram up close to your nose until you begin to lose focus. Allow your eyes to relax for a bit.
2. Then, slowly pull the stereogram away again. Try not to refocus on the image; instead, continue to relax and "space out."
